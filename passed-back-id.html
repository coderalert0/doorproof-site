What is a passed-back ID?

Checked against its sources on November 7, 2026

Short answer

A passed-back ID is a real, unexpired card used twice in one night. Someone of age gets in, then hands their ID out the door to a friend who looks enough like them. The card passes every check because it's genuine, so what catches it is noticing the same card a second time.

A borrowed ID, delivered on the spot

Virginia ABC sorts fake identification into three kinds: false IDs that were manufactured, altered IDs with changed details, and borrowed IDs, valid cards used by someone else. A passed-back ID is a borrowed ID handed over at the door the same night, often through a window, a smoking area or a friend walking back out.

Why a hand check misses it

Everything about the card is real. The holograms, raised print, barcode and dates all check out, and the birth date is old enough. The only clues are the person:

  • The photo is close but not quite. Compare the nose, chin, ears and eyes, which makeup and hairstyles don't change.
  • The height doesn't match.
  • They hesitate over the address, middle name or birth month.

California and Virginia both teach asking those questions when something seems off. See how to check an ID at the door.

How venues catch it

  • Hand stamps and wristbands. Marking guests who are in makes a second entry harder, but a stamp doesn't follow the card.
  • A second staff member watching the line. Someone who notices a guest step back out and return can stop the hand-off.
  • A record of tonight's scans. When every ID is scanned, a second scan of the same card in one night stands out. A scanner that keeps that record can say when and at which door the card was first seen, so staff know to look closely at the person in front of them.

Frequently asked questions

Is using someone else's ID illegal?

California lists unlawful use of a driver's license or ID card among its false ID offenses, and Virginia counts a borrowed ID as fake identification. Check your own state's law. The person who sells or serves to a minor can face penalties too.

Can a scanner tell that an ID belongs to someone else?

Not from the barcode alone. A borrowed card scans clean. A scanner can only flag that the same card was already used tonight, and staff then compare the photo and ask questions.

Should I check IDs again inside if they were checked at the door?

Virginia ABC recommends it: an ID checked at the door can still be checked again at the bar, because the licensee answers for every drink served.
