## Before you start: get the card in your hand

Ask the guest to take the ID out of their wallet or holder. California and Virginia both recommend it. You can't feel a card through plastic, and Virginia notes you may spot a second ID in the wallet.

## F: Feel

- **Rigidity and thickness.** A fake often weighs and flexes differently from a real card.
- **Edges.** A real card's edges are smooth and rounded all the way around. Lifted corners can mean the card was split and re-laminated.
- **Bumps and ridges.** Run a thumb over the photo and the birth date, where information is most often cut out and pasted on.
- **Raised print.** Some states laser-engrave features you can feel. California's cards have a raised signature and a raised date of birth.

## L: Look

- **Do the math.** Count the age from the birth date, or read the "under 21 until" date many states print. California notes that decoy stings often catch staff who asked for ID but never checked the age.
- **Expiry.** An expired ID isn't acceptable ID in California or Virginia.
- **Photo.** Hairstyles, makeup and beards change. The shape of the nose, chin, ears and eyes doesn't. Virginia suggests covering facial hair in the photo to compare.
- **Height and description.** The height, and in some states the weight, should reasonably match the person.
- **Typeface and spelling.** Computer-made fakes can have wrong fonts, mixed sizes or spelling errors.
- **Orientation.** AAMVA's card standard specifies a vertical card for drivers under 21. California and Virginia print under-21 cards vertically, so a vertical card at a 21+ door deserves a careful look.

## A: Ask

If anything seems off, ask questions. The real holder answers without thinking.

- Their license number, middle name or ZIP code.
- Their birth month. California and Virginia both note that someone who answers with a number instead of the month's name may be lying.
- Their zodiac sign or the year they finished high school.
- Their companion's name for them, asked of the companion quickly.
- A signature to compare with the one on the card.

A second ID with the same name can settle it. Virginia suggests comparing a credit card or another card with the name on it.

## G: Give back, or refuse

If the card passes, give it back. If it doesn't, refuse the sale or entry. What you may do with a suspected fake differs by state:

- **California** lets a licensee seize a suspected false ID. Staff must give the person a receipt and turn the ID over to local police within 24 hours.
- **Virginia** treats the ID as the holder's property. Staff must return it if asked, and only law enforcement may keep it.

Check your own state's rules and your venue's policy before keeping any card.

## Where a scanner fits

A scanner does the math and reads the barcode in a second, which catches expired, under-age and altered cards at a busy door. It can't feel edges or see holograms, and a well-made fake scans clean. Use the scan to decide where to look, then finish with FLAG. See [do ID scanners detect fake IDs?](do-id-scanners-detect-fake-ids.html)

## Frequently asked questions

### What does FLAG stand for when checking IDs?

Feel, Look, Ask and Give back. It's the routine California's Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control and Virginia ABC teach staff who check IDs.

### What IDs are acceptable for buying alcohol?

It depends on the state. California accepts a valid government-issued card with the name, birth date, photo and physical description, including out-of-state licenses, military IDs and US or foreign passports. Virginia's list includes state driver's licenses, military IDs and US passports. Expired IDs, student IDs and Social Security cards aren't acceptable in either state.

### Can I keep a fake ID?

In California, yes, with a receipt to the person and the card handed to police within 24 hours. In Virginia, no: return it if the person asks, and call law enforcement. Other states have their own rules.

### Why are some driver's licenses vertical?

AAMVA's card design standard specifies a vertical layout for drivers under 21, so staff can spot an under-21 card at a glance. Many states follow it.
